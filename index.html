I've spent the last three years getting reality into computers by hand — photogrammetry of a centuries-old Kyoto temple, AR interfaces for lunar vehicles, Gaussian-splat capture experiments. The question I keep coming back to: when do learned world models replace hand-built reconstruction — and what does reconstruction still know that generation doesn't? That thread — capture → reconstruction → learned simulation — connects everything below.

Projects

NASA SUITS — AR interface for the Lunar Terrain Vehicle

AR UI/UX on Columbia Space Initiative's LTV team (NASA Johnson Space Center student challenge). I designed the state color-coding system astronauts read at a glance under lunar constraints — offline, gloved hands, narrow field of view — and built an error-code → actionable-instruction translation feature, integrated into the team's shared Unity architecture.

Team repo · Unity · 2026

Recruit × Carsensor — new-business plan & prototype

Summer 2026 product-management internship at Recruit (Tokyo) on Carsensor, Japan's largest used-car marketplace. Business model aimed at cutting the used-car selling journey from ~30 days to same-day, with a working prototype combining 3D vehicle scanning, AI exterior-damage detection, and smartphone sensor data. Grand prize at the final presentation to Carsensor's Vice Presidents.

Product management · prototyping · Jun–Aug 2026

Kyoto Taizō-in — XR reconstruction

Photogrammetry-based 3D reconstruction of Taizō-in, a 600-year-old Zen temple in Kyoto, brought into VR — digital preservation so the temple can outlive its wood. The project that started the world-models thread.
